### A young French girl is thrown from a train in Cornwall; investigation ensues.

***

*For Braddon, see Noovels* [*004*](/blog/2017/12/12/novel-004-me-braddon-the-doctors-wife)*,* [*061*](/blog/2019/1/15/novel-061-me-braddon-lost-for-love-1874)*,* [*115*](/blog/2020/1/28/novel-115-me-braddon-aurora-floyd-1863)*,* [*170*](/blog/2021/2/16/novel-170-mary-elizabeth-braddon-only-clod-1865)*,* [*222*](/blog/2022/2/15/novel-222-mary-elizabeth-braddon-birds-of-prey-1867)*,* [*223*](/blog/2022/2/22/novel-223-mary-elizabeth-braddon-charlottes-inheritance-1868)*,* [*280*](/blog/2023/6/24/novel-289-me-braddon-to-the-bitter-end-1872) *and* [*334*](/blog/2025/7/19/mary-elizabeth-braddon-strangers-and-pilgrims-1873)*.*

The plot "in the highest degree exciting"; *Wyllard’s Weird* is "an exceedingly clever and skilfully-told tale"; Braddon is "one of the few writers who combines with the power of inventing an intricate plot the art of painting real men and women with marked and distinct individualities.  Almost every one in the novel is a person whom we might have met, and whom we could never have mistaken for a marionette or a ghost.” *Saturday Review*, April 25, 1885

“The book has a great charm, and it is that of the olden time, when Miss Braddon was the mark for imitation.” *Spectator*, May 2, 1885
