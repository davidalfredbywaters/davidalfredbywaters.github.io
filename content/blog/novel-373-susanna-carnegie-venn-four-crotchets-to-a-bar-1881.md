### A doctor's wooing of a rich merchant's beautiful daughter is bedeviled by her four maiden aunts and various scheming inhabitants of their seaside town

***

*Susanna Carnegie Venn (1843-1931), wife of the Cambridge mathematician after whom the Venn diagram is named, wrote five novels between 1876 and 1894; this one amply exhibits her tireless wit.*

“This is an excellent specimen of the better kind of trashy novel. . . .  There is no elaborate character-drawing, and no elaborate plot-making, but it is full of a lively knowledge of the world, and of rattle almost always amusing and now and then very clever.”  *Spectator*, July 2, 1881

“A novel which may be praised”; it is “lively enough, not particularly original in plot, but told with ability and a considerable sense of fun.” *Athenaeum*, August 13, 1881

*A contrasting view:*

“There is a certain cleverness in this story, but we greatly doubt whether it is enough to make up for the vein of low farce which runs through it. . . .  Almost all the characters are very vulgar”; “With all its faults . . . it is not nearly so bad or so poor a novel as many . . .  at Mudie’s.”  *Saturday Review*, July 9, 1881
